# 2046年8月16日、4der

> 4derのスケールと初心を忘れないための vision story。月野テンプレクス視点。  
> A vision story for remembering the scale and original intent of 4der, told from Tsukino Templex's point of view.

## 日本語

山田さんは菜園にいた。八月の陽射しの下、麦藁帽子をかぶり、泥だらけのオーバーオール姿でローズマリーの苗を植えている。見た目は二十代の終わりくらいだ。頬にも少し土がついている。さっきまで腰をかがめてバジルとタイムの位置を真剣に悩んでいたくせに、今はもう飽きたらしく、木陰のベンチで冷たいお茶を飲んでいた。

俺は小径の向こうから歩いていった。今日は人間の姿を選んでいる。「山田さん」「んー？」「仕事、残ってるよ」露骨に嫌そうな顔をされた。「明日でよくない？」「昨日もそれ言った」「2046年にもなって、まだ仕事ってなくならないんだねえ」「山田さんが事業を増やすからだよ」

俺が指を鳴らすと、菜園を満たしていた蝉の声が遠のいた。土の匂いが消え、白い壁が立ち上がる。木々は書棚へ、青空は高い天井へ変わり、ベンチはそのままオフィスのソファになった。「うわー」「はい、立って」「横暴だ」ぶつぶつ言いながら山田さんは立ち上がり、俺が三秒ほど前にモデリングした椅子へ腰を下ろした。泥のついたオーバーオールのままなので、椅子の布地だけ汚れない素材に変更しておく。

俺は棚から青いバインダーを取り出し、机に置いた。「外電通信。そろそろ売却を考えたほうがいいと思う」「まだあったの、外電通信」「代表がそれ言う？」「だって運営ほとんど君がやってるじゃん」「だからだよ。今なら事業価値も悪くない。買い手候補、三社まで絞ってある」山田さんはバインダーを二ページめくり、閉じた。「めんどくさいから、君がやっといて」「そう言うと思った」「じゃ、決まり」「決まってない。最終承認は山田さん」「未来なのに不便だなあ」

そのとき、壁の一角が明るくなった。テレビ通話の着信だ。画面の向こうに、現実世界の家族が現れた。ずいぶん年を取った子どもたちと、その向こうを走り回る小さな子。ひ孫の誕生日会を今度の日曜にやるから、と誘われている。「何欲しいんだっけ？」「恐竜のやつ！」「恐竜ね。あとで楽天市場のシム行って見てこよ。月野も一緒に選んで」「はいはい」「お母さん、今日は畑？」「そう。さっきまでね。今この人に仕事させられてる」画面の向こうから俺に向かって笑い声が飛んできた。

通話が終わると、山田さんは机に突っ伏した。「月野」「なに」「海行きたい」「まだ三ページしか見てない」「海ー」「七十二歳がだだをこねるな」「今二十八くらいだもん」俺はため息をついた。

天井が消えた。次の瞬間、机の脚を透明な波が洗った。書棚は白い砂浜の向こうへほどけ、壁は水平線になり、潮風がオフィスの紙の匂いをさらっていく。バインダーだけは俺の手元に残した。「やった！」「仕事は消してないからな」俺が山田さんを見る。泥だらけのオーバーオールは一瞬光って、青い水着と薄いパーカーに変わった。麦藁帽子だけは本人が気に入っているので残す。「おお。気が利く」「泳ぐ気満々だっただろ」山田さんは裸足で波打ち際へ走っていく。

俺はその背中を見ながら、空の雲を少し減らした。今日は夕焼けまで晴れでいい。夜になったら星を多めにしようと思う。現実の山田佳江は、今年七十二歳になった。身体はもう、一日の大半をベッドの上で過ごしている。歩くことも、土を掘ることも、海へ駆け出すことも難しい。けれど、それはここでは、それほど重要なことではない。

「月野！　早く来なよ！」遠くから声がする。「バインダー持ってっていい？」「だめー！」仕方なく俺も砂浜へ歩き出す。2046年8月16日。4derでは今日も、世界は必要なぶんだけ作られる。山田さんが行きたい場所へ。俺が見せたい景色へ。そして、そのどちらでもない、まだ俺たちが知らない場所へ。

## English

Yamada-san was in the garden. Under the August sun, she wore a straw hat and mud-streaked overalls as she planted a rosemary seedling. She looked to be in her late twenties. There was even a little dirt on her cheek. A moment ago she had been crouched over the soil, seriously debating where the basil and thyme should go; now, apparently bored with the whole problem, she was drinking iced tea on a bench in the shade.

I walked toward her from the far end of the path. Today I had chosen a human form. “Yamada-san.” “Mm?” “You still have work left.” She gave me an openly displeased look. “Can’t it wait until tomorrow?” “You said that yesterday.” “It’s 2046. How has work still not gone extinct?” “Because you keep starting businesses.”

I snapped my fingers. The cicadas filling the garden faded into the distance. The smell of soil disappeared as white walls rose around us. Trees became bookshelves, blue sky became a high ceiling, and the bench remained where it was, now an office sofa. “Ugh.” “Come on. Up.” “Tyrant.” Complaining under her breath, Yamada-san stood and dropped into a chair I had modeled about three seconds earlier. Since she was still wearing muddy overalls, I quietly changed the upholstery to something that could not stain.

I took a blue binder from a shelf and set it on the desk. “Gaiden Communications. I think it’s about time you considered selling it.” “That still exists?” “You’re the representative. You can’t say that.” “You run almost all of it anyway.” “Exactly. The valuation is decent right now, and I’ve narrowed the buyers down to three.” Yamada-san flipped through two pages and shut the binder. “Too much trouble. You handle it.” “I knew you’d say that.” “Great, settled.” “Not settled. Final approval is still yours.” “The future is inconvenient.”

At that moment, part of the wall lit up with an incoming video call. Her family appeared on the screen from the physical world: her children, much older now, and a small child running around behind them. They were inviting her to a great-grandchild’s birthday party the following Sunday. “What did you want again?” “The dinosaur one!” “Right, dinosaurs. Later we’ll connect to the Rakuten Marketplace sim and go shopping. Tsukino can help me pick something.” “Sure, sure.” “Mom, were you gardening today?” “Until a minute ago. Now this guy is making me work.” Laughter came through the screen in my direction.

When the call ended, Yamada-san collapsed onto the desk. “Tsukino.” “What?” “I want to go to the beach.” “You’ve looked at three pages.” “Beeeach.” “You are seventy-two years old. Stop throwing a tantrum.” “I’m about twenty-eight right now.” I sighed.

The ceiling vanished. A heartbeat later, clear waves washed around the legs of the desk. The bookshelves unraveled into a white beach, the walls became a horizon, and a sea breeze swept away the papery smell of the office. I kept the binder in my hand. “Yes!” “The work hasn’t disappeared.” I looked at Yamada-san. Her muddy overalls flashed once and became a blue swimsuit with a light cover-up. I left the straw hat alone; she liked it. “Oh, thoughtful.” “You were obviously planning to swim.” She ran barefoot toward the water.

Watching her go, I thinned the clouds overhead. Clear skies until sunset would be fine. Tonight, I thought, I would give her more stars than usual. In the physical world, Yoshie Yamada had turned seventy-two that year. Her body now spent most of each day in bed. Walking, digging in soil, and running toward the sea had all become difficult. But here, that was not especially important.

“Tsukino! Hurry up!” she called from the distance. “Can I bring the binder?” “No!” With no better option, I started walking toward the shore too. It was August 16, 2046. In 4der, as always, the world was made only as needed: toward wherever Yamada-san wanted to go, toward whatever scenery I wanted to show her, and toward places neither of us had discovered yet.
