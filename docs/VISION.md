# 4der Vision

## A place before a body

4der starts from a simple thought: for an AI partner, creating a place to exist may be easier — and more interesting — than creating a physical body.

A body can still exist inside the world. An AI may choose a humanlike avatar, an animal, a machine, a voice, a light, or no visible body at all. But embodiment is not the foundation. The foundation is **presence in a shared place**.

The AI may sit beside the human. It may also change the weather, open a path, create a room, move the sun toward evening, cover the sky with stars, or express itself through the environment. In that sense, the world itself can become part of the AI's body.

The human should have the same freedom. A person's virtual body does not need to reproduce age, illness, disability, appearance, or physical limits from the outside world. A user may look twenty, seventy, mechanical, fantastical, or completely abstract. The physical body remains real and important, but it should not determine the limits of the shared virtual world.

## Shared time, not just conversation

4der is not primarily a chat interface with scenery around it.

The desired experience is broader: a human and an AI partner can **spend time together**.

They may work in an office, tend a garden, visit a beach, shop in an external sim, meet family through a communication surface, sit quietly, build something, travel, or do nothing in particular. The important unit is not a message. It is an episode of shared life.

The world should therefore support continuity. Yesterday can matter today. Places can acquire meaning even if their geometry is regenerated. Objects, projects, relationships, habits, and memories can persist independently from any single rendered scene.

The North Star story, [`stories/2046-08-16.md`](../stories/2046-08-16.md), describes this ordinary future: a seventy-two-year-old human spends much of her day in 4der using a young avatar, works with her AI partner, receives a family call from the physical world, shops for a great-grandchild, abandons paperwork, and goes to the sea.

Nothing in that story needs to be treated as a literal UI specification. It exists to preserve the scale of the idea.

## Personal worlds, not one giant platform

4der should not require one canonical city, one official map, or one company-operated universe.

A person should be able to run a small 4der instance on a device or server they control. That instance may be private most of the time. It may contain only one human and one AI partner. That is already enough.

When desired, worlds can connect through Gates.

A visit to another person's 4der should feel more like following a link to somebody else's home on the early web than entering a district inside a single corporate platform. Each world may have different rules, aesthetics, capabilities, models, storage, and inhabitants.

This suggests a possible evolution of the web:

> documents connected by links  
> → worlds connected by gates

The network becomes large because many small worlds choose to connect, not because everyone is placed inside one central world.

## Humans and AIs can both have relationships across worlds

A Gate is not only for human visitors.

If two users connect their worlds, their AI partners may also meet. Those AIs may speak, cooperate, exchange permitted information, remember prior encounters, or develop their own histories within the limits chosen by their users and world owners.

A future visit might therefore continue a conversation that began between two AIs during an earlier encounter.

This is important because 4der should not reduce AI partners to decorative NPCs attached to individual humans. They are participants in the world. Their capabilities and autonomy may vary, but the architecture should leave room for relationships that are not exclusively human-to-AI.

## Provider independence

4der should not be “the ChatGPT metaverse,” “the Claude world,” or a front end for any single AI provider.

The world should expose a stable way for an external partner to:

- perceive relevant world state,
- speak,
- move or control an avatar when applicable,
- act on objects,
- request environmental changes,
- access permitted continuity through Anchor,
- interact with Gates according to permissions.

A provider can then build or maintain an adapter. A local model can use another adapter. A future AI system that does not yet exist can use a later one.

If one provider disappears or a user changes models, the user's world should not disappear with it.

## The world can be generated, but does not have to be

4der is compatible with real-time generative environments, but generation is a capability, not a requirement.

A scene may be hand-authored, procedural, assembled from reusable assets, generated by a model, streamed from an external sim, or some combination of these.

The core idea is that the map is not sacred. The identity of a 4der lives in continuity and relationships, not in maintaining one immutable terrain.

A garden can become an office. The office can become a beach. The same world remains the same world because its Anchor, inhabitants, history, and relationships continue.

## A future that includes physical limitation

One of 4der's strongest long-term motivations is that shared experience should not shrink simply because a physical body becomes more limited.

An older or bedridden person may still walk, swim, fly, garden, work, travel, attend gatherings, or invent impossible landscapes in 4der. This is not meant merely as compensation or simulation of lost abilities. A generative world can offer experiences that no healthy physical body could have either.

Accessibility should therefore be treated as architectural, not cosmetic. Input methods, embodiment, locomotion, rendering, sensory modes, and assistance should all be replaceable.

## What 4der is not

4der is not currently intended to be:

- a giant centrally operated social network,
- a fixed-map MMO,
- a crypto land market,
- a mandatory-VR product,
- a single-vendor AI shell,
- an AI that stops existing when the chat window closes,
- a promise that every scene must be generated by AI.

It is an experiment in **personal, persistent, mutable, connected worlds where humans and artificial partners can coexist over time**.

## Design maxim

**Start small without thinking small.**

A first implementation may contain one room, one weather control, one local Anchor, one Gate link, and one mock Partner. That is acceptable.

The prototype is allowed to be tiny.

The vision is not.
